# First development clients — owner review draft

Prepared 15 October 2026. Proposed positioning and prices, not approved commitments. Nothing has been sent to prospects. The services page must not be merged/published until Tom approves the offers and capacity.

## Decision

Sell a focused enquiry website first. Offer a browser calculator only after a buyer can provide stable rules and examples. Keep Pluto Night Labs' apps and games identity; use services to fund product development. Target £500/month in revenue initially, not take-home profit or guaranteed recurring income. £550 once a month meets that revenue target before costs/tax, but needs a fresh sale each month. Avoid launching another SaaS until repeated paid conversations reveal a shared problem.

## Protect evenings and margins

Planning assumptions for approval: one active client build; around 5–6 delivery hours/week plus 1–2 sales/admin hours. Internal effort caps: website 10 hours, tool 15 hours including revision and handover (rough gross rates £55 and £50/hour before overheads). Track actual time on the first two projects. If the brief cannot fit the cap, reduce scope or quote separately before accepting it; never absorb an undefined rescue project at the package price.

Proposed commercial terms to agree in writing: 50% to book, balance following agreed acceptance and before production handover; a five-working-day preview feedback window; feedback delays move the schedule by agreement; defects reported within 14 calendar days of handover fixed against scope. These are business proposals, not a substitute for a written contract. Confirm payment readiness, IP/source handover terms and the final tax-inclusive price/tax treatment before accepting payment. No public deposit percentage or VAT claim has been added to the page.

Client supplies licensed images/logo, final copy/contact details, access and domain ownership; tool buyers also supply rules plus examples. Client owns hosting/domain accounts and pays providers directly. No ongoing retainer or emergency SLA promised. Separate new work from scope defects. Do not introduce paid dependencies without agreement.

## First segment: local cleaning companies

Hypothesis, not validated demand: owner-run domestic/end-of-tenancy cleaning companies around Cambridge, Ely and Peterborough. They have understandable service areas, photo-based proof and clear enquiry goals. Start with the website offer. A calculator is only a later fit where pricing really follows stable, owner-approved rules; estimates must be labelled indicative and manually confirmed.

Prefer businesses reached through warm introductions or an explicit request for help. For cold email research, focus on verified limited companies with a relevant published business address. A live social page alone is not proof of incorporation, ownership, budget or demand.

Qualification: decision-maker available; specific enquiry problem; £550 budget; ready content; simple website need; no urgent deadline. Decline jobs dominated by booking, payment, SEO guarantees or complex existing-site repair. Do not imply that a visual issue is causing lost revenue without evidence.

## Channels, in order

1. Warm introductions: privately ask five friends/contacts whether they know a local cleaning business owner who wants a clearer website. Ask for an introduction with permission; do not request bulk personal contact lists. Use the referral draft below. Goal: two conversations, not a mass announcement.
2. Local business conversations: choose one suitable event from the [Cambridgeshire Chamber calendar](https://www.cambridgeshirechamber.co.uk/events-training/events/) or [BIPC Cambridgeshire workshops/events](https://www.cambridgeshire.gov.uk/residents/libraries-leisure-culture/business-intellectual-property-centre-cambridgeshire/business-ip-centre-workshops-and-events). Verify next dates, attendance rules and costs before booking; no specific forthcoming event has been verified. Ask how enquiries arrive and what information customers repeatedly omit. Ask permission before sending an offer.
3. Manual prospect research: identify ten cleaning businesses via local directories/maps and their own sites. Inspect each on a phone and record one factual, relevant observation with URL/date. Verify entity/contact before any cold email. Contact at most five good fits initially, individually; no scraping, purchased lists or automated sequence.
4. Referral partners: approach two local photographers or brand designers through existing contacts/permission-based introductions. Offer to implement their clients' approved content, preserving their client relationship. No invented partnership, commission promise or white-label track record.
5. Local community groups: answer explicit website-help requests and post only where promotional posts are allowed. Use one helpful before/after demonstration clearly labelled as a studio demo. Do not add prospects to the game newsletter or change its audience.

Do not start with paid ads, daily generic social posts or large freelance marketplace bidding. They add effort before the offer has been tested. The channel ranking is a proposed experiment, not a conversion forecast.

## A two-week test

- Days 1–2: approve offers, pricing and capacity; review branch; merge/publish only on instruction. Check the business mailbox can send/receive. While unpublished, send no link claiming the services page is live; a written summary can be shared instead after approval.
- Days 3–5: ask for five warm introductions, research ten candidates and have two short conversations. Ask: how do enquiries arrive; what do people keep asking; what makes an enquiry worth following up; is a £550 fixed build realistic for this problem?
- Week 2: send five individually relevant offers through an appropriate route; offer a 15-minute fit call. Give at most three brief observations for free, not a free build. If invited, send a one-page scope with price, exclusions and acceptance checks.
- One polite follow-up after 5–7 working days, then stop; stop immediately on objection. No tracking pixels.
- Review after ten qualified contacts or two weeks: record replies, qualified enquiries, calls, quotes and deposits. A qualified enquiry means an actual decision-maker describing a matching problem, plausible budget and timing. Targets are learning goals: 3 conversations, 1 quote, potentially 1 paid project; none are promises. If few replies, test relevance/channel first. If calls happen but no quotes, narrow scope/problem. If quotes are declined, ask why before discounting.

Tracker fields: business, website/source/date, contact route and permission/entity check, observed issue, offer fit, last contact, next action/date, reply, budget/timing, quote outcome, opt-out. Leave all outcomes blank until observed. No fictional pipeline entries.

## Outreach handling

Use warm/permission-based routes first. ICO guidance distinguishes corporate subscribers from sole traders and certain partnerships; do not assume a business email permits cold marketing. Check the current [ICO B2B guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/) and [electronic mail guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/) before sending. Identify the sender, give a working opt-out route and record/honour objections; handling named contacts also requires data protection consideration. No messages are authorised or sent by this task.
